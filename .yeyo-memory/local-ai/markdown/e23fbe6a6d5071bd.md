## Storage
Please note that all the instructions on storage refer only to the pump! The instructions regarding the storage of other purchased parts (e.g. motor, mechanical seals, thermo-siphon equipment, etc.) must also be complied with!

### Requirements for the storage area:
- The pumps must be stored in a frost free place.
- The storage area must be roofed over, dry and safe against flooding.
- With closed rooms care should be taken to ensure that they are well ventilated.
- „Pump / aggregate“ must be protected against impact and shock loads, e.g.caused by objects, tumbling down.

### Storage and Maintenance Instructions:
- The storage-position is optimally, if corresponding to installation for operation; that means vertically and adequately supported at the sole plate of the delivery elbow. If vertically storage is impossible, the pumps must be stored in the horizontal position (lying down) and adequately supported. This means that pumps with a long submersion depth must be supported every 2m to prevent them from sagging.
- The pumps must also be adequately fixed in their location
- All openings on the pump must be covered so that neither dust, water nor dirt can enter.
- On metal pumps or plastic pumps having metal components in the hydraulic section of the pump (e.g. impeller, volute casing, suction cover etc.) the following additional maintenance instructions must also be complied with.
  - Remove the covers from the suction and discharge nozzles every 4 - 8 weeks and check the pump visually for corrosion damage.
  - Smear the sealing faces of the suction and discharge nozzles with a suitable preservative.
  - If possible, and permitted by the user, spray the hydraulic section of the pump through the suction and discharge nozzles with a suitable preservative which is compatible with the product and the material. Then reseal the suction and discharge nozzles.
- For horizontal pumps: Check the pump about every 8 - 12 weeks for external corrosion damage and rotate the shaft 3 - 4 times by hand. For vertical pumps this time-interval is reduced up to 3 – 4 week.
- On pumps which have gland packing and which have been in storage for longer than 3 months, the gland packing should be removed and replaced with new packing material before the pump is put into operation, since the gland packings can deteriorate with age.

If it should turn out that a pump is being stored for a period of more than 5 years, the pump should be completely disassembled and checked for damage to individual components. If the pump is subject to excessive pollution during storage such as for example due to dust / dirt, aggressive vapours and/or the effects of severe weather etc., it should be completely disassembled after 3 years and checked for damage to individual components!

Note 1: If there is any doubt when making the assessment RHEINHÜTTE should be informed or you should have the assessment carried out by RHEINHÜTTE Service! Both the lubricants in the anti-friction bearings and also the gaskets and sealing rings can be subject to changes depending on the resistance of the material to ageing and could adversely affect the operating safety of the pump. They should thus be replaced. The anti-friction bearings should be washed out, dried, inspected for damage and then refilled with the specified grease.

Note 2: The necessary instructions regarding the lubrication of the bearings are attached to the Operating Instructions and must be complied with! Gasoline, petroleum spirits, dewatering fluids, neutral and alkaline aqueous cleaning fluids can be used for cleaning the anti-friction bearings. Dangers to health and risk of accidents when using the above cleaning agents! When using one of these cleaning agents the pump user must take all necessary measures to protect personnel!

Before putting the pump into operation all threaded fasteners (all nuts and bolts accessible from outside) must be checked and re-tightened in accordance with the document "Torque loadings for headless screws" attached to the Operating Instructions and if necessary also the document "Torque loadings for reduced shaft screws".

## Product Description
### Type and design description – pump type: GVSO ...(“Molten Salt” - version)
(3.09.5100-002-Rev.05_EN)
#### General:
The RHEINHÜTTE vertical centrifugal pump type GVSO generally consists of a submerged tank pump (dry installation is possible to a limited extent. Please consult RHEINHÜTTE). The pump casing with double volute is normally (for wet installation) submerged in the liquid to be handled and is connected to the mounting flange by the suspension pipe. The pump is driven by a vertical flanged motor mounted on top of the motor stool.
Depending on the pump size, the shaft is supported by one or more intermediate bearings (depending on the depth of submersion) for submersion depths greater than approx. 2200 mm. The column and suspension pipes consist of the discharge pipe (for the medium) and the shaft column. This means that the rotating shaft is not exposed to the flow.
There are overflow holes in the upper part of the delivery suspension pipe which prevent the medium rising through the sleeve bearing gap from reaching the shaft entry.
The overflow holes in the delivery suspension pipe should be above the level of the liquid in the tank at all times and be kept open!
The shaft entry does not come into contact with the pumped medium.
The support bearings in the volute and stage casing (for multi-stage pumps) and the intermediate bearings are lubricated by the pumped medium, provided this is free from solid particles.
In this version (“Molten Salt” - version), the machine is not approved for use in gas-explosive atmospheres or dust-explosive atmospheres!
#### Identification markings:
Each pump unit features a rating plate on the bearing bracket. The customer is advised to note the sales order number (R..) and serial number (P..) and to quote these in relation to all enquiries and when ordering spare parts from Rheinhütte.
These numbers must be quoted in all correspondence and all orders for spare parts.
#### Design features:
The design depicted is explained in the Technical Data Sheet describing the pump in detail.
##### Shaft seal options:
In the case of molten salts, depending on the requirement, the following shaft sealing models come into the reckoning:
- **Model A with stuffing box**: This type of sealing is used in pumps that are built in tanks, in the case of which there is a small differential pressure to the atmosphere, as well as for deployment against gasifying and fuming media. Between the packing rings, there is a locking ring, which can be charged, depending on the requirement, with compressed air or nitrogen (20 – 50 mbarg). The consumption is 60 – 80 Nl (standard litres) / min
- **Design Y**: The shaft opening is sealed by one or several lip rings or by a pressurized carbon floating ring seal. This design can only be used if the shaft guide pipe is not subject to pressure. With a carbon floating ring seal, container pressures up to max. 4 bar can be sealed by air or nitrogen
##### Direction of rotation:
The direction of rotation of the GVSO is anti-clockwise (to the left) when viewed from the drive end shaft stub. Exceptions are the pump sizes 200/400, 250/400 300/400, 400/500 und 450/500 which rotate in a clockwise direction (to the right)
The drive unit should always be disconnected from the pump when checking the direction of rotation!
The direction of rotation of the motor should correspond to that of the pump. Always check the direction arrow on the pump!
##### Thrust bearing lantern:
Depending on the load (i.e. depending on the size and speed), the shaft is supported by one or two radial bearings in the thrust bearing lantern in the case of GVSO pumps.
Under heavy pump operating conditions, the bearings have a nominal service life of 20,000 hours (assuming that the pump is operated correctly). Bearings are lubricated with grease. It is important that radial bearings are installed which have increased clearance corresponding to “C3”.
##### Drive shaft:
Pumps with intermediate bearings have a split shaft. The joint of the shaft units and the transmission of the torque are achieved by means of a sleeve coupling which also acts as a shaft sleeve in the intermediate bearings.
##### Mounting flange:
The mounting flange is circular, unless required in some other form. It is sealed with a gasket. The flange joining dimensions of the sole plate (144) as well as of the pressure pipe flange are defined in these cases on a case-to-case basis and should be taken from the dimensional arrangement drawing. The flange size indicated on the dimensional drawings (installation drawing) corresponds to the inside diameter.
Corrosion resistant pumps usually have a plated mounting flange.
##### Foot bearing, intermediate bearing:
In case of molten salts, the lubrication of the sleeve bearing is done by the transported material. Lubricant is applied via holes from the pressure pipe and the suspension tube filled with conveyed medium.
##### Design for installation in tanks:
In this design the suction flange on the pump is not drilled. In the suspension pipe there are overflow holes through which the rising liquid drains back into the tank.

# Installation Instructions
## General
- Remove protective covers before installing the pump.
- Install vertically; support flange on tank/pit bearing surface, aligned to max. 1 mm/m with a level.
- Avoid vibration and running dry by not allowing liquid to fall below minimum level.
- Increase submersion depth slightly with an extension pipe (discuss with RHEINHÜTTE).
- Ensure liquid level above impeller center line for startup.
- Use suction tube carefully, avoiding cavitation.
- Control liquid level regularly; install monitoring device if necessary.
- Provide flowmeter at discharge nozzle when discharging tank.
## Installation Site
- Ensure sufficient space around pump for easy access.
- Install equipment in similar fashion before or after the pump.
- In closed rooms, provide ventilation to avoid heat buildup and ensure fresh air supply.
- Protect pump/aggregate from shock loadings.
## Piping
- Size and routing of pump pipework specified at design stage.
- Ensure tank distance prevents flow disruption; discuss with RHEINHÜTTE if necessary.
- Discharge pipe diameter should match pump discharge connection or be larger (discuss with RHEINHÜTTE).
- Provide isolating valve next to pump for pressure and/or flow rate regulation.
- Recommend check or non-return valve between isolating valve and discharge nozzle.
- Pressure gauge on discharge side must be fitted between outlet connection and isolating valve.
- Connect pipes only after thorough cleaning, precise alignment, and proper pump positioning.
- No subsequent welding or alignment work allowed once pump connected to pipes.
## Gaskets
- Gaskets must not foul pipe and create adverse flow conditions.
- Material suitable for operating conditions in installation.
- Smeared with suitable grease if permitted by pumped medium and gasket material.

# Tightening Torques for Stretch Bolts

The information contained in this chapter is intended to assist with the correct way of dealing with stretch bolts used on RHEINHÜTTE metal pumps. If anything is still unclear or if problems with stretch bolts are anticipated or even occur on our products, RHEINHÜTTE Div. Rheinhütte Pumpen should always be informed! Further activities, e.g. assembly, commissioning, should then be stopped until the matter is resolved!

## Introduction
If normal bolts are continually subjected to alternating loads they will break after some time as a result of fatigue even when they are adequately strongly designed. In these cases stretch bolts (resilient bolts) are much better able to withstand the loadings. Their shank diameter is only about 90% of the root diameter of the thread (0,9 x d3). The "necked", i.e. thinner, shank takes up the tensile stresses, which would otherwise overload the thread.

Working alternately across the joint stretch bolts must be tightened/pre-stressed evenly within the elastic limit to specified figures (torque loadings) using a suitable torque wrench. A bolt is correctly tightened when it has been strained below the yield point, i.e. in the elastic zone of the bolt material. The pre-tension force acting on the bolt causes it to stretch, pressing the bolted components together. If the bolt is tightened too little the pre-tension force will be too low. This will allow the bolt to work loose. If it is properly tightened to just below the yield point, the pre-tension force will reach the right level. If the bolt is tightened too much it will be permanently overstrained and the pretension will be lower. Long bolts are less at risk because they can stretch more than short bolts.

The torque applied when assembling a bolted joint must give a pre-tension force which is high enough to maintain the minimum clamping force when subject to operating forces. The pre-tension forces and torque loadings in the table are calculated so that the tensile stress in the cross section which matters (the cross sectional area of the stretch shank) together with the torsional stress which occurs when tightening reaches 90% of the 0,2% proof stress.

- The liquid level in the tank is too low
- Pipe-work, impeller or valves choked, clogged or encrusted
- The actual operating parameters are greater than stated on the order
- The geodetic height or the frictional resistance of the pipe is greater than originally assumed
- The suction opening on the pump is too close to the bottom or is in the sludge
- Suction strainer too small or blocked

### 7.1.3 Too much delivery from the pump and/or pump does not run smoothly!
Possible cause:
- Running speed is greater than stated in the order, the flow rate is too high
- The pressure required in the installation is lower than stated in the order, the flow rate is too high
- Suction strainer is too small or blocked
- Coupling pack worn
- Bearing damage
- Impeller blocked or out of balance due to wear
- Pump is sporadicly suctioning air

### 7.1.4 Pump leaking!
Possible cause:
- Bolted joints not properly tightened
- Damage to various parts around the shaft exit or on the can
- The drain holes in the column pipe are blocked
- Return pipe wrongly laid

### 7.1.5 Motor overloaded!
Possible cause:
- The operating data does not agree with the pump design data
- Base bearing / intermediate bearing damaged
- Stuffing box too tight
- Specific gravity or viscosity of the product being pumped is greater than stated
- Dirt in the hydraulic components of the pump

### 7.1.6 Impermissible temperature rise in the pump / increased bearing temperature!
Possible cause:
- Pump runs against a closed shutoff valve
- Pump runs dry
- The actual operating parameters differ from those stated on the order
- The geodetic height or the frictional resistance of the pipe is greater than originally assumed
- Delivery rate too low

# Metastream™ TSKS Kupplung

## Einbau-
Nach dem Entfernen der Verpackung ist die Kupplung sorgfältig auf Beschädigungen zu überprüfen. Achten Sie hierbei besonders auf die Nabenbohrungen und Anschlußzentrierungen, die weder Grat noch Beschädigungen aufweisen dürfen.

## Nabenmontage
Arbeiten an der Kupplung dürfen nur im Stillstand ausgeführt werden. Das Antriebsaggregat muss gegen unbeabsichtigtes Einschalten gesichert werden. Die Naben sind während der Montage ausreichend gegen Herabfallen zu sichern.

Stellen Sie sicher, daß die Wellenenden und Bohrungen der Kupplungsnaben sauber sind.

### Zylindrische Bohrung mit Paßfeder
1. Überprüfen Sie Wellendurchmesser und Nabenbohrung auf die korrekte Passung.
2. Montieren Sie die Paßfeder(n) in die entsprechende Wellennut und ziehen, bei Vorliegen einer Spielpassung, die Kupplungsnabe auf die leicht geschmierte Welle auf. Die Paßfeder sollte hierbei fest in der Wellennut sitzen und ausreichend Rückenspiel aufweisen. Richten Sie die Innenseite der Kupplungsnabe bündig mit dem Wellenende aus. Die axiale Sicherung der Kupplungsnabe erfolgt in der Regel durch einen Gewindestift auf der Paßfeder.
3. Liegt eine Übergangs- oder Presspassung vor, kann ein Erwärmen der Kupplungsnabe notwendig sein. Ein warmes Ölbad ist hierbei normalerweise ausreichend. Vermeiden Sie eine punktuelle Erwärmung bzw. Temperaturen größer 175° C, da es hierdurch zu Verformungen kommen kann!
Richten Sie die Nabe wie zuvor beschrieben aus.

### Kegelbohrung mit Paßfeder ABB. 2
1. Die Kupplungsnabe ohne Paßfeder auf die Welle schieben und mit geeignetem Werkzeug auf den Wellenkegel pressen.
2. Mit einem Tiefenmikrometer den Abstand vom Wellenende bis zur Nabeninnenseite messen und mit dem entsprechenden Sollmaß in der Ausführungszeichnung abgleichen.
3. Wenn nötig, die Nabe entfernen und die Paßfeder(n) einsetzen. Die Paßfeder sollte hierbei fest in der Wellennut sitzen und ausreichend Rückenspiel aufweisen.
4. Nach dem Einsetzen der Paßfeder(n), die Nabe wieder soweit auf die Welle schieben bis der zuvor gemessene Abstand zwischen Wellenende und Nabeninnenseite erreicht ist. Siehe Abb. 2. Beachten Sie bitte, daß bei kegeligen Bohrungen Nabeninnenseite und Wellenende nicht bündig sind.

### Naben mit Ölpressverband
1. Stellen Sie sicher, daß Nuten und Radien der Paßflächen, die Ölverteilung und Ablaufrinnen gut abgerundet sind und keinen Grat aufweisen.
2. Alle Kontaktflächen sind zuvor gründlich zu säubern und die kegligen Flächen mit Öl zu schmieren.
3. Schieben Sie die Nabe auf die Welle.
4. Montieren Sie die Ölpumpe und das entsprechende Montagezubehör gemäß Ausführungszeichnung. Beachten Sie bei der Montage die Anweisungen des entsprechenden Herstellers.

Die Hydraulikmutter befestigen und sichern, BEVOR Öl eingespritzt wird!

5. Öl einspritzen, bis der gewünschte Druck erreicht ist oder Öl zwischen den Kontaktflächen austritt.
6. Mit Hilfe der Montagewerkzeuge die Nabe in die richtige, axiale Position bringen. Hierbei weiterhin Öl einspritzen.
7. Öldruck ablassen.
8. Ölpumpe und Montagezubehör entfernen.
9. Wenn vorgesehen, Sicherungsscheibe und Sicherungsmutter am Wellenende montieren.

Vor dem Bohren der Kupplungsnabe, ist diese auszurichten. Als Bezugsflächen dienen hierbei zum einen die innere Stirnfläche der Kupplungsnabe, zum anderen der Zentrieransatz. Siehe Abb. 3

## Adapter
Bei Maschinen, die über eine integrierte, geflanschte Welle verfügen, sollte der Flansch so bearbeitet werden, daß der Lochkreis des Flansches dem Lochkreis der Transmissionseinheit entspricht. Andernfalls kann die Kupplung mit einem kundenspezifischen Flanschadapter geliefert werden.

## Wellenausrichtung
Die Achsen der treibenden und der getriebenen Wellen wie folgt ausrichten:
1. Antriebsaggregat und Arbeitsmaschine aufstellen.
2. Beide Maschinen grob ausrichten und anschließend die Arbeitsmaschine fixieren.
3. Den Abstand zwischen den Wellenenden (DBSE) durch Verschieben des Antriebsaggregats nach Zeichnung herstellen.

WICHTIG! Für die Ausrichtung muss der Abstand zwischen den Innenflächen der Naben gemessen werden. Das Abstandsmaß der Nabeninnenseiten ist der entsprechenden Ausführungszeichnung zu entnehmen und muß nicht unbedingt mit der Länge der Transmissionseinheit identisch sein. Weiterhin kann, z.B. bei kegeligen Bohrungen, dieses Abstandsmaß von dem eigentlichen Wellenabstand abweichen. Siehe Abb. 4.

4. Die horizontale und vertikale Ausrichtung der Wellen erfolgt mit Hilfe der Nabenflansche. John Crane empfiehlt für eine genaue Ausrichtung das Umkehrverfahren. Dieses Verfahren kann mitverschiedenen Meßinstrumenten durchgeführt werden. Je genauer die Ausrichtung der Maschinen erfolgt, umso größer ist die Lebensdauer der Kupplung. Wir empfehlen daher die Verwendung von einem lasergestützten Meßsystem, z.B. das John Crane Wellenausrichtungssystem "Lase-A-Lign".
5. Nach dem Ausrichten der Maschinen, ist der Abstand zwischen den Nabenflanschen nochmals zu kontrollieren und gegebenenfalls korrekt einzustellen.
6. Bei der Verwendung von Kegelverbindungen kann es zu Schwierigkeiten beim Einstellen des genauen Wellenabstandes kommen. In diesem Fall können auf Anfrage axiale Ausgleichsbleche geliefert werden.
