## Purpose and scope
This document is intended to evaluate the implications of replacing the existing TES System plate heat exchangers, for a new set of heat exchangers of the tube and shell based on Lointek’s H&MB 4521-CAL-LOI-039-062-0002-GAR. The new proposed heat exchangers will have different pressure drops and will be located at a different location and at heights of the existing ones. To evaluate the consequences of installing this equipment on the Molten Salts and Heat Transfer (HTF) circuits, a set of hydraulic calculations were simulated using Pipeflo®. The considered cases were the following:

- For the Molten Salts Circuit:
  - Summer Charging & Steam Generation
  - Summer Charging Only
  - Summer Discharging & Steam Generation
  - Winter Discharging Only
  - Max Charging & Steam Generation
  - Max Charging Only
  - 25% Charging & Steam Generation
  - 25% Charging Only
  - Design Charge Operation with Interchangeability (*)
  - Design Discharge Operation with Interchangeability (*)
- For the HTF Circuit:
  - Summer Charging
  - Summer Discharging
  - Winter Discharging
  - Max Charging
  - 25%

## Molten Salts Circuit Description & Considerations
The plant includes 6 identical and independent molten salts circuits. For simulation purposes the following equipment where considered:

- One (1) hot salts tank (1/../6 -MT-252)
- One (2) cold salts tank (1/../6 -MT-251)
- One (1) HTF-to-Salt Heat Exchanger (1/../6 -MX-251)
- One (1) hot salts pump (1/../6 -MP-252)
- One (1) cold salts pump (1/../6 -MP-251)

Because there are no differences in terms of geometry and equipment performance, the molten salts circuit was simulated for a single set of tanks for the different operating modes.

## Hypothesis and Assumptions
The following assumptions have been considered for the calculations:

- Pipes length and fittings details based on the 3D latest Model and P&IDs collection.
- Salts tanks operating pressure: 14.5 psia.
- Cold molten salts temperature:
  - Charge mode : 556 °F
  - Discharge mode: 556 °F and 564 °F for the Normal and Winter Discharge modes, respectively.
- Hot molten salts temperature: 730 °F.
- Pumps suction height : 1 ft
- Heat Exchanger Inlet nozzle height:
  - Discharge mode: 37,49 ft
  - Charge mode: 16,67 ft
- Heat Exchanger Outlet nozzle height:
  - Discharge mode: 16,67 ft
  - Charge mode: 37,49 ft
- Heat Exchanger pressure drop: based on the vendor’s Heat & Mass Balances
- Pumps performance curve: as specified in its datasheet.
- Control valve’s Cv based on vendor’s datasheet

# HTF Circuit Description & Considerations

The HTF circuit is a closed system with backpressure ensured by the expansion tank. It consists of 10 identical main pumps (2 in standby), sending cold HTF to the solar field and through 6 identical HTF-to-Salts heat exchangers, followed by two steam generators.

For simulation purposes:
- Ten (10) HTF Far Field Pumps (MP-200 A/B/C/D/E/F/G/H/J/K) - 8 running, 2 spare
- Pumps suction pressure: 172 psia (set by the expansion tank)
- Six (6) HTF-to-Salt Heat Exchanger (MX-251)
- Two (2) identical steam generators in parallel

## Hypothesis and Assumptions

- Pipes length and fittings details based on the latest 3D Model and P&IDs collection
- Cold HTF Temperature: ranging from 545 °F to 598°F
- Hot HTF temperature: ranging from 718 °F to 738°F
- Pumps performance curve as specified in datasheets
- Solar field designed with induced pressure drop
- Temporary strainers at main pumps suction removed
- Heat Exchanger pressure drop based on vendor’s Heat & Mass Balances
- Operating flows as indicated on initial heat exchangers datasheets
- Far field pumps operation considerations: 8 out of 10 running, equipped with VFDs
