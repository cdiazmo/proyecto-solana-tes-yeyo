# 4521-MEM-AEE-68-10-0001-GAR Rev 00 TES Replacement HEX Operation Philosophy

## Resumen

- Replacement of HEXs in TES system
- New tube & shell type HEXs for improved performance
- Installation of a drain back tank
- Interchangeability between TES systems
- Adjustments to the HTF circuit
- Charge operation heats cold M S with hot HTF from solar field.
- MS circuit must be purged of gases before normal operation.
- Shutdown involves stopping pumps and opening vent valves to drain MS.
- Discharge operation uses the same procedure as charge but with hot MS pump.
- Drainage is an exceptional mode for maintenance work on MS circuit.

# Operation Philosophy / Operation Modes
## Charge Operation
The charge operation consists of heating the cold M S using hot HTF from the solar field. The salts are pumped from the cold MS tank and then sent to a HEX, to be stored in the hot MS tanks.
## MS Circuit Description
Between normal charge and discharge operations the MS circuit will not be totally drained and there will be gases accumulation. As a good engineering and operation practice, before starting the normal operation the circuit must be purged of all gaseous pockets trapped inside it. With the new MS circuit, there are two (2) high points in the circuit, located at the hot and cold MS jumpers (where the gases shall accumulate). At these high points there are two vents, each with an on/off valve and connected to the cold salts tank.
To evacuate these gases and fill the whole MS circuit, the cold salts pump is set to a minimum speed and the receiving tank (hot tank) inlet valve is closed, whilst the vent valves are open. The flow of MS should push all gases. This procedure is finished as soon as through the vents there are MS returning to the tank. Once there are salts flowing through the vent, the vent valves are closed and the hot salt tank inlet valve opened and the system set to automatic mode.
## HTF Circuit Description
Once the MS circuit is filled with liquid the control valve on the hot and cold HTF open and the HTF flows to heat the cold MS.
## Shutdown / Stand-by
MS Circuit Description
When the receiving tank level (hot or cold) is reaching its maximum level, the running pump starts reducing its speed until is fully stopped. Once there is no MS flowing through the system both the hot and cold salt tanks inlet lines valves open, as well as the vent valves. By doing this it is possible to drain all the MS inside the circuits in the platform area (before the jumper) back to the MS tanks.
As for the MS circuit section that is at a height below the tank inlet height, it will remain inside the circuit and kept above its freezing temperature with the help of the electrical tracing system.
HTF Circuit Description
As soon as the charge or discharge operation is stopped the control valves, that connect the cold and hot HTF circuits to the TES system, close and the circuit is kept “bottled” until a new charge or discharge operation is performed. Likewise for the MS circuit, to prevent from freezing the HTF the electrical tracing system is set on.
## Discharge Operation
The same procedure as described for the Charge Operation for the HTF and MS circuits. But instead of running the cold MS pumps, it is the hot MS pump running.
## Drainage
This operation mode is an exceptional situation for the plant, it should be performed only if the installation requires maintenance works that don’t allow for the MS circuit to be filled (e.g.: changes of valves or HEX repairs).
MS Circuit Description
Operator shall ensure that TES tanks pumps are stopped and there is enough spare volume at cold MS tank to receive them. Open the vent valves at jumpers to drain back tank, to avoid any vacuum inside the pipes. After that, open drainage valves at HEX system to allow discharge to drain back tank and empty HEX. Once level at drain back tank get a fixed set point, the pump inside the drain back tank shall start. The operation shall continue until a minimum level is reached inside the drain back tank, meaning that the MS circuit is drained. The drain back pump stops as well as the MS drainage valves are closed. Vent valves are kept open to maintain the circuit blanketed.
HTF Circuit Description
There is no automatic drainage operation for the HTF circuit. In case the system is drained, the procedure is done manually and when the HTF is cold enough for a safe operation.
