# 1950201-PPM-001

## Resumen

- The document is titled 'MAGNETIC PARTICLES PROCEDURE' and is owned by Abengoa.
- It outlines the procedure for Magnetic Particle Testing in welded joints, edges, and metallic material of a project.
- The procedure requires qualified personnel at least as Level 2 to evaluate indications.
- The document references ASME Code, Section V and other standards for non-destructive examination.
- There is a revision history detailing updates to the procedure.
- The procedure requires requalification for essential variable changes and revision for all other changes.
- Equipment includes AC yoke with a pull force of 4.5 kg and UV-A lamp with specific wavelength and intensity.
- Wet particles must be fluorescent or non-fluorescent, while dry particles require specific temperature limits.
- Examination involves applying magnetic particles on the surface, using continuous method for both wet and dry particles.
- Field strength must be checked with a Berthol sample to ensure appropriate magnetizing field.
- Multiple magnetic fields should be applied to guarantee 100% coverage of the surface.
- The procedure involves magnetic particle examination for detecting surface and subsurface discontinuities in welds and components.

## 5.0 EQUIPMENT

- The equipment for magnetization will be of AC yoke that produce longitudinal magnetization. The yoke must have a pull force of 4,5 kg at the maximum pole spacing used.
- A UV-A lamp with a wavelength of 320-380 nm and intensity not lower than 1000 μW/cm² will be used with fluorescent particles.

Light meters shall be calibrated at least once a year or whenever the meter has been repaired. If meters have not been in use for one year or more, calibration shall be done before being used.

## 6.0 MAGNETIC PARTICLE PRODUCTS

### 6.1 WET PARTICLES

These particles shall be fluorescent to be examined with black light or non-fluorescent to be examined with white light, immediately before applying particles, the suspension shall be agitated to guarantee an uniform fluid dispersion.

Visible (color Contrast) Magnetic Particles:
- Ardrox 800/3. Black Particle.
- Ardrox 8901W: Lacquer for Contrast Enhancement when it is necessary.

Fluorescent Particles:
- LUMOR J. Highly Sensitive Fluorescent magnetic ink.

The temperature of the surface to be examined shall be between 5ºC to 52ºC.

### 6.2 DRY PARTICLES

The operator must be sure that the powder used is adequate for the temperature of the piece and that it is dry enough.

Colour contrast dry powders:
- Ferromor ND-8 Yellow. Surface temperature limit: 200ºC.
- Ferromor ND-5 Grey. Surface temperature limit: 300ºC.

All particles shall be used within the temperature range limitations set by the manufacturer of the particles. Alternatively, particles may be used outside the manufacturer's recommendations with the approval of the Quality Control Manager and they will be demonstrated to the satisfaction of A.I.

Another similar products could be used with the approval of the Quality Control Manager and they will be demonstrated to the satisfaction of the A.I.

When is necessary to measure the intensity of the residual magnetic field, a magnetism indicator must be used.

## 7.0 DESCRIPTION OF THE PROCESS

### 7.1.1 SUPERFICIAL CONDITION

Before the magnetic particle examination, a check of the examination surface shall be conducted to locate any discontinuity surface opening which may not attract and hold magnetic particles because of their width.

The weld to examine and adjacent areas within at least 25 mm, will be dry and clean, free of all dirt, grease, lint, scale, welding flux and spatter, husk, oxide, welding projections, oils, machining marks or any other strange materials that can interfere with the exam or the interpretation.

### 7.1.2 MAGNETIC PARTICLE TECHNIQUES

The examination shall be done by the continuous method. The method consists on the application of magnetic particles of very small size on the surface of the piece.

**DRY PARTICLES:**

The magnetizing current shall remain on while the examination medium is being applied and while any excess of the examination medium is removed. Accumulations of excess dry particles in examinations shall be removed with a light air stream from a bulb or syringe or other source of low pressure dry air.

**WET PARTICLES:**

Wet particles shall be applied before magnetizing current is applied. If the wet particles are applied from aerosol spray cans or pump sprayers, may be applied, before and/or during magnetizing current application.

Visible Magnetic particles:
- The minimum light intensity shall be 1000 lux on the surface to be examined. The light intensity shall be measured with light meter prior to the evaluation of indications.
- If the contrast of the surface is not appropriate for the inspection a Lacquer for Contrast Enhancement will be applied.
- Nonmagnetic surface contrast may be applied by the examiner to uncoated surfaces, only in amounts sufficient to enhance particle contrast. When nonmagnetic surface contrast enhancement is used, it shall be demonstrated that indications can be detected through the enhancement.

Fluorescent particles:
- It shall be performed in a darkened area (the visible light will be less than 20 lux).
- Operators shall be in a darkened area for at least 5 min prior to performing the examination to enable their eyes to adapt to dark viewing.
- UV-A lamp must be used with a wavelength of 320-380 nm and an intensity not lower than 1000 μW/cm². Reflectors and filters shall be checked and, if necessary, cleaned prior to use. Cracked or broken filters shall be replaced immediately.

### 7.1.3 GENERAL REQUIREMENTS

Field strength shall be checked with a Berthol sample, having two lower cross section grooves. If two grooves are clearly seen, the magnetizing field shall be considered appropriate to perform the exam.

The exam will be made applying at least two magnetic fields on the same area following the table 1 to guarantee the 100% exam of the surface. The applied magnetic field shall have sufficient strength to produce satisfactory indications, but shall not be so strong that it causes masking of relevant indications by non-relevant accumulations of magnetic particles. Factors that influence the required field strength include the size, shape and material permeability of part, etc.

During the second examination, the lines of the magnetic flux shall be approximately perpendicular to those used during the first examination. All examinations shall be conducted with sufficient overlap to ensure 100% coverage.

When the residual magnetism can interfere with the phases of production or final work conditions, the piece will be demagnetized.

When post-examination cleaning is required, it should be performed prior to later processes.

## 8.0 EVALUATION
An indication is the evidence of a mechanical imperfection. Only indications with major dimensions greater than 1.5 mm shall be considered relevant.
- Linear indications are those having a length exceeding three times the width of the indication.
- Rounded indications are those which are circular or elliptical and have a length of less than three times the width of the indication.
- Any doubtful or questionable indication will be checked again to verify if it is relevant.
When two adjacent indications are separated by a lower distance than the bigger dimension of the smallest one, they should be evaluated as a single continuous indication.

## 9.0 ACCEPT / REJECT CRITERIA
All surfaces to be examined shall be free of:
- Cracks like indications detected irrespective of surface conditions (ASME VIII Div 2 only)
- Relevant linear indication.
- Relevant rounded indications greater than 5 mm.
- Four or more relevant rounded indications in a line separated by 1,5 mm. or less (edge to edge).
According with ASME B31.1
- Any cracks or linear indications
- Rounded indications greater than 5 mm
- Four or more relevant rounded indications in a line separated by 2 mm. or less (edge to edge)
- Ten or more rounded indications in any 3870mm² of surface with the major dimension of this area not to exceed 150mm with the area taken in the most unfavorable location relative to the indications being evaluated
Only indications with major dimensions greater than 1,5 mm shall be considered relevant.

## 10.0 FINAL REPORT
The result of the exam will be documented with the corresponding report that will include as minimum the following data:
- Job number
- Procedure identification and revision.
- Drawing.
- Identification of the weld, part or component examined.
- Magnetic particle equipment and current used
- Magnetic Particles type
- NDE operator qualifications
- Map or record of relevant indications
- Material and thickness
- Lighting equipment
- Date of examination
- Evaluation
- Acceptance criteria

Describir la sistemática para la detección de discontinuidades abiertas a la superficie y subsuperficiales en soldaduras y partes de componentes, utilizando el método de magnetización mediante yugo. # PROCEDIMIENTO GENERAL DE PARTÍCULAS MAGNÉTICAS

**5.0 EQUIPAMIENTO**
- Equipos de magnetización: corriente alterna, fuerza para elevar una pesa 4.5 Kg.
- Lámpara UV con longitud de onda 320-380 nm y intensidad no menor de 1000 μW/cm².
- Luxometro calibrado anualmente o después de reparación.

**6.0 PRODUCTOS PARTÍCULAS MAGNÉTICAS**
- Partículas húmedas: fluorescentes para luz negra, no fluorescentes para luz blanca.
- Partículas secas: polvo con color contraste, temperatura límite en superficie.

**7.0 DESCRIPCION DEL PROCESO**
- Inspección superficial antes de examen por partículas magnéticas.
- Técnica de partículas magnéticas: método de campo continuo.
- Partículas secas: aplicación mientras circula la corriente de magnetización.
- Partículas húmedas: aplicadas antes de magnetizar la pieza.

**7.1.3 Requerimientos generales**
- Intensidad del campo magnético comprobada con indicador tipo cruz de Berthol.
- Examen mediante aplicación sucesiva en cada área, garantizando el 100% del examen.

## 8.0 EVALUACIÓN
Una indicación es la evidencia de una imperfección mecánica. Sólo se considerarán relevantes aquellas indicaciones cuyas dimensiones superen los 1.5 mm.
- Indicaciones lineales son aquellas en las cuales la longitud es mayor que tres veces la anchura.
- Indicaciones redondas son aquellas de forma circular o elíptica cuya longitud es menor que tres veces la anchura.
- Cualquier indicación dudosa o cuestionable será comprobada nuevamente para verificar si es o no relevante.

Cuando dos indicaciones adyacentes están separadas por una distancia inferior a la mayor dimensión de la más pequeña de las dos indicaciones deben tratarse como si fuera una sola indicación continua.

## 9.0 CRITERIOS DE ACEPTACIÓN
Todas las superficies examinadas deberán estar libres de:
- Grietas, independientemente de las condiciones (ASME VIII Div 2 solo)
- Indicaciones relevantes lineales
- Indicaciones relevantes redondas mayores de 5 mm.
- Cuatro o más indicaciones relevantes redondas en línea separadas por 1,5 mm. o menos (borde a borde)

De acuerdo a ASME B31.1:
- Cualquier grieta o Indicaciones relevantes lineales
- Indicaciones relevantes redondas mayores de 5 mm.
- Cuatro o más indicaciones relevant es redondas en línea separadas por 2 mm. o menos (borde a borde)
- Diez o más indicaciones relevantes redondas en una superficie de 3870 mm² con un área que no excederá los 150 mm en la localización más desfavorable

Solo las indicaciones con dimensiones mayores de 1.5 mm serán consideradas relevantes.

## 10.0 INFORME FINAL

- Material y espesor.
