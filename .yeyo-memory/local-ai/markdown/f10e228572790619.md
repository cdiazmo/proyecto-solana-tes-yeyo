# Installation, Start-Up, Operation and Maintenance Instructions
## Section 1: Precautions
- Heaters should be plugged into a cover with a nitrogen nozzle.
- Nitrogen pressure should be maintained at 0.1 bar for uniform function.
- Do not attempt to remove covers or plugs from pressurized vessels.
- Bleed nitrogen pressure down to zero before removing gasket cover bolts.
## Section 2: Installation Instructions
- Each Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger is shipped assembled and ready for installation on purchaser's supports.
- Install heater in a true horizontal plane as shown on the General Arrangement Drawing.
- Review Transport drawing for lifting information (empty weight, center of gravity).
- Use nylon slings with rubber protection for contact points if using steel slings.
## Section 3: Handling Instructions
- Two hook lift is suitable for horizontal vessels only.
- Tilt vessel lengthwise to move through narrow areas.
- Slings should be sized at 1.4142 times the vessel weight.
- Avoid striking into objects and dropping or bumping during handling.

## Heat Exchanger System Rigging
- Proper rigging must be used for lifting assembled Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger Systems.
- Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger Systems are heavier than pressure vessels of comparable dimensions; weights should be secured from the Transport Drawing.
## Setting Heaters
- Fixed supports require shims for proper elevation and orientation.
- Foundation bolts in slotted support holes must be loosened for free expansion of the shell.
- Anchor bolt stakeout must match equipment drill distribution.
- Sliding support stainless steel plate ensures top elevations align with fixed support nuts.
## Equipment Alignment
- All bolt elements (washers, bushings, nuts, locknuts) must be correctly installed before lifting.
- Saddle elevation must be the same for upper and lower equipment.
- Gauges (0.2-0.08 inch) ensure correct leveling; sliding support gauges are stainless steel, fix support carbon steel.
## Piping Connections
- Remove shipping covers and plugs immediately prior to connections.
- Residual water in the heater must be removed through nozzles.
- Personnel must caution against visual inspection of internals due to nitrogen's colorless and odorless nature.
- All connections on General Arrangement Drawing must be utilized unless otherwise approved by LOINTEK Engineering Department.
- Pipelines should be blown before welding to equipment.
## Corrosion Protection
- Oil to Salt Heat Exchangers are protected against corrosion with nitrogen; heater should remain sealed until piping is complete and protected immediately afterward.
## Alterations or Repairs
- Any alterations or repairs during installation, testing, or operation must be made under LOINTEK direction and in accordance with LOINTEK procedures. Contact LOINTEK for necessary instructions.
## Hydrostatic Testing
- If field hydrostat testing will be performed, see ANNEX 2.
## Removal of Welded Shipping Covers
- LOINTEK covers weld end nozzles with steel caps to protect the weld end and offer a stable nitrogen seal.
- Bleed internal nitrogen pressure to zero before cutting off shipping cover.
- Grinding is the intended method of removal; start at the edge of the cap and break the cap to remove the weld joint.

# Start-Up and Operation Instructions for Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System

## Section 2: START-UP AND OPERATION

Starting, operating, and shutting down of an Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System require specific instructions, with emphasis on load limitations and the order of start-up and shut-down. The plant operating instructions and the Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System Drawing, specification sheet, and nameplates are to be reviewed prior to operating the system.

### Precautions
Improper starting-up or incorrect shutdown sequence can cause leaks in tube-plate weldings, flanged joints of equipment, or even a tube breakage.

### Operation Modes
- **Initial Start-Up (Filling)**
- **Warm Start-up (Normal Start Up)**
- **Hot Stat-up & Standby mode**
- **Shutdown**

## 2.1 Initial Cold Start-Up

During the initial start up of the plant and following extended scheduled maintenance periods, the heat exchangers will be subjected to the following sequences:

- Heat exchangers are initially empty at ambient temperature.
- Electric heat tracing on the heat exchanger shells and channels is activated until 50ºC with a heating rate of 1,8ºF/hour (1ºC/hour).
- When shells and channels reach 176ºF (80ºC), preheated HTF at 176ºF (80ºC) is introduced.
- Gradually increase HTF temperature with a maximum ramp of 90ºF/hour (50ºC/hour) up to 545ºF (285ºC).
- Introduce salt at ± 27ºF (15ºC) from the previous established temperature.

## 2.2 Warm Start-Up (Normal Charge or Discharge)

For normal charge or discharge modes, follow the operation limits specified in 4521-MOM-LO-039-062-0003-GAR.

Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System operating conditions and performance should be checked regularly against the values stated on the specification sheet provided for each heat exchanger. Any significant deviation of heater performance from that specified should be immediately reported to LOINTEK.

## 2.3 Shutdown

The shutdown of the oil to salt heat exchangers system must first stop the hot fluid, then stop the flow on the cold side.

When taking an exchanger out of service for an extended period, provisions should be made to protect the internals from corrosion and pitting by blanketing both sides with a positive pressure of nitrogen or another inert gas. If this is not possible and freezing conditions will not be encountered, both sides should be flooded with oxygen-free condensate.

## DRAIN

The first fluid to drain should be salts. There is no limitation in pressure differences between fluids apart from design pressures. The temperature ramp must not exceed 18ºF/minute (10ºC/min), measured by the average of the drain outlet temperatures. Nitrogen must be introduced at the highest points of the exchanger to facilitate the drain of the salt.

The heat exchangers are supplied with a % slope to facilitate this operation.

## 3.2. SHELL REMOVAL AND REPLACEMENT

**SCOPE:**

These instructions cover the removal and replacement of tube bundles in an Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System.

**GENERAL:**

Care must be exercised to ensure that the bundle is always evenly supported.

Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System bundles are heavy, but individual parts, such as tubes, are relatively thin and easily damaged. Care must be exercised at all times to ensure that there is no sudden shift in the bundle or binding that can result in damage to the tubing. A large number of tubes can be easily damaged with one wrong move during the removal or insertion of a bundle.

**SHELL REMOVAL:**

The shell is pulled off using pulling lugs.

The shell is pulled in steps, pausing between each step to provide support for the bundle. Cribbing to raise the weight of the bundle off the shell before clearing the last support plate so the bundle end will not drop and strike the shell as support under the bundle is removed.

On horizontal heaters, cribbing or other adequate support must be placed at the maximum spacing of 2m. Cribbing must be long enough to carry the full width of the flat bottom of the support plates. Bars or pipe tacked to the support plates and extending to the floor or some other adequate arrangement must be provided to ensure against any overturning of the bundle.

The suggested practice is to employ a beam-type monorail with plain trolleys to cover the required number of support points.

A chain hoist or "come along" chain pull is recommended with each trolley to permit fine adjustment of the bundle height at each support point. This is important on bundle removal and critical on bundle insertion.

It is preferred that exposed area of the bundle and all shell openings be covered with plastic or other suitable material as soon as the shell is removed in order to minimize corrosion and accumulation of dirt, etc.

**REINSTALLATION OF SHELL (BUNDLE INSERTION):**

Prior to insertion, a thorough inspection is required. The bundle must be checked for alignment. The bundle must be level, straight, and there should not be any twist between the forward and rear ends. The bundle and internal surfaces of the shell must be clean.

The shell is drawn over the bundle by placing a cable around the forward shell support and pulling from the channel end. After the shell is approximately halfway over the bundle, the cable can be shifted to a support further back, if required.

**NOTE:** THE PULLING CABLE SHOULD BE LIFTED UP ON THE SUPPORT AS CLOSE TO THE SHELL AS POSSIBLE WHEN PULLING, TO MINIMIZE ANY BENDING MOMENTS ON THE ATTACHMENT OF THE SUPPORT TO THE SHELL.

An air operated hoist is generally used for bundle insertion. However, come-alongs or any other suitable device may be used. A mechanic must be stationed on each side of the shell to observe the open end of the shell passing across support plates and bundle skids. No interference can be permitted. Bundle support cribbing is removed as required. Note that, when stopping to remove cribbing, the nearest bundle skid and/or support plates should be inside the open end of the shell.

The bundle insertion should be monitored at the shell opening to assure support plates do not catch the shell and bend. Remove the rigging only as the tube bundle is supported by the shell as it is inserted.

**CLOSURE SEAM FIT-UP AND WELD:**

Fit-up lugs may be tack welded to the shell to aid in pulling the shell over the back-up strip and final positioning.

**NOTE:** FIT-UP LUGS, ETC., SHOULD BE WASHED OFF BY AIR ARC AND GROUND SMOOTH. THESE LUGS "SHOULD NOT BE KNOCKED OFF". AFTER FIT-UP, CHECK ALIGNMENT AND LOCATION OF PRINCIPAL CONNECTIONS FROM REFERENCE "ZERO" ON THE SETTING PLAN DRAWING.

**RADIOGRAPHIC EXAMINATION:**

Radiographic examination is required on carbon steel seams having a thickness of 1-1/4" and greater.

**HYDRO TEST:**

On completion of the welding of all connections, a hydro test should be conducted in accordance with the Hydro Test section of these instructions.

## ASSEMBLY
All bolted connections are to be tightened uniformly in a diametrically staggered pattern as shown on the Bolt Tightening Pattern Sketch. Tightening to the listed torque values shall be achieved in multiple steps as noted below, with each tightening increment performed in the same diametrically staggered pattern. This will ensure a uniform stress over the entire gasket surface.

1. The joint shall be snuggled up squarely so the entire flange face bears uniformly on the gasket.
2. Tightening of the bolts shall be applied in at least three equal increments to achieve torque values listed in the following torque table. It is suggested that 4 equal increments be used for bolt diameters above 2”.
3. Alternatively, bolts can tighten one flat at a time in a diametrically staggered pattern until the specified torque is achieved.

If the bolted connection leaks, the nuts can be tightened in the pattern outlined on the Bolt Tightening Pattern Sketch, one flat at a time. The spacing between the flanges must be equal throughout the tightening process. This will ensure the gasket surface will not be distorted.

If a connection continues to leak, it must be disassembled and examined for the following:
- Indication of a leak path, such as a gouge across the gasket surface.
- Evidence of interference between the mating connections.
- Evidence of distortion or out of roundness of either of the mating gasket surfaces. This is usually best checked by measuring the gasket compression every 45 degrees around the gasket itself. Thickness measurements should also be taken close to both the I.D. and the O.D. of the compressed portion of the gasket to check against any possible rotation of the flange during bolt tightening.
- Evidence of bolt diameter reduction or "necking", which would indicate an overstressing of the bolts.

In order to ensure that all bolted flanged joints remain tight, such joints should be tightened uniformly immediately after the heater has been placed in service for the first time, and again after the heater has been operated at full load for the first time. It is recommended that periodic checks be made during the first six months of operation to ensure proper tightness of all bolted joints.

## LOCATING TUBE FAILURES
To determine the probable causes of tube failure, it is essential that the exact location of each failure be known. This instruction gives a method of locating the point of failure in an Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger tube. Although other means may be employed, it has been our experience that the system described represents a practical procedure for making an accurate determination of the location of a tube failure.

When collecting data on tubing failures, the following minimum amount of information must be recorded:
1. LOINTEK Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System "Serial Number".
2. The pass in which the failure occurred (inlet or outlet).
3. Tube row, counted from pass plate.
4. Tube number, counted from the left when facing the tubesheet.
5. Distance of the failure back from face of tubesheet.
6. Length of failure, where possible.
7. Date of investigation.

TUBESHEET/TUBE END EXAMINATION:
The face of the tubesheet, the tube ends and the inlet pass tube inside surfaces are to be visually examined using a bright light, from a distance of approximately 30cm. This examination should disclose any pitting, "bug holes", or other indications of damage.

If any tubes have been previously plugged, they must be checked to ensure no leak paths have developed around the plug.

Once the visual inspection is finished, it is recommended to make a penetrant test in the tubesheet surface. With this test it is possible to detect damages in the weldings between the tube and the tubesheet.

Other testing will be performed in order to find damages inside the tubes like bubble test.

# ANNEX 2: HYDROSTATIC TESTING

**LIMITATIONS:**
- Hydrostatic test pressures of closed Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger Systems are stated on each heat exchanger's nameplate and on the General Arrangement drawing.
- Under no circumstance is the hydrostatic test pressure to be exceeded. VERIFY SHELLSIDE AND TUBESIDE TEST PRESSURE STAMPED ON THE OIL TO SALT HEAT EXCHANGER SYSTEM NAMEPLATE PRIOR TO ANY FIELD HYDROTEST.
- Hydrostatic testing using a water temperature of less than 60 OF (15.5 OC) is not recommended. Check ASME code to calculate the minimum temperature to be used.

**AFTER FILLING:**
- Hold for approximately one-half hour, drain a sample and check the temperature to ensure that it is higher than the minimum recommended hydrostatic test temperature.

**CODE INSPECTION:**
- When hydrostatic testing is conducted to demonstrate acceptability of repairs performed on pressure parts of Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System, it is recommended that these tests be witnessed by the owner's Authorized Inspector and Notify Body.

**TESTING PRECAUTIONS:**
- During filling, start-up vents must be opened and remain open until all passages have been purged of air or nitrogen and are completely filled with fluid.
- After completely filling the chamber to be tested and purging it of air or nitrogen, the vents are to be closed.
- Pressure is to be applied at a steady rate. In no case should the vessel be subjected to a sudden pressure surge. It is recommended that a relatively small capacity pump be used to apply hydrostatic test pressure.
- It is recommended see the hydrostatic test drawing before starting the test.

**DRAINAGE AFTER HYDROSTATIC TEST:**
- Following hydrostatic testing, the Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System is to be drained. It is preferred in most cases (and essential in the case of carbon steel heaters) that they be dried and nitrogen blanketed following drainage.
