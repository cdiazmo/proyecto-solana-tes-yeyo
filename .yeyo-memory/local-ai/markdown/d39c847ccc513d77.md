## Important: Read Before Installing

The instructions given in this manual are intended to aid you in the operation, performance and preservation of your new LOINTEK Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System. While everything discussed in this instruction manual is important, we have selected these pages to emphasize several instructions that must be followed when installing or operating your Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System.

1. All parts painted orange must be removed prior to testing or operating. See the “Transport Drawing” in order to know which part is in orange colour (temporal supports, hydraulic test covers,…). Penetrant or magnetic test will be required after removing these parts.
2. Check the tightness of all permanent plugs and covers prior to pressurizing the heater.
3. LOINTEK must be notified prior to performing any warranty work.
4. A pressure gauge on an Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System indicates the vessel is filled with nitrogen gas for shipping. ALL PERSONNEL MUST BE CAUTIONED NOT TO PUT THEIR HEAD INTO VESSEL OPENINGS. NITROGEN IS COLORLESS AND ODORLESS AND SUFFOCATION COULD TAKE PLACE WITHOUT THE SENSES PROVIDING AN ALARM.
5. Proper caution must be taken to assure that the Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System has been drained and vented and all flow has ceased, prior to entering either the channel or shell side of an Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System.
6. To insure that all bolted joints remain tight, such joints should be pulled up evenly after the unit is put in service under a full operating load. On all bolted flange joints (not pipe connections), it is suggested that insulation be left off, or that a removable insulation blanket be used.

Any significant installation, operation, or maintenance problems should be reported to LOINTEK.

# SECTION 1: SHIPPING AND INSTALLATION

## 1.1. PRE-INSTALLATION STORAGE OF OIL TO SALTS HEAT EXCHANGER SYSTEMS

**CAUTION:** A PRESSURE GAUGE ON AN OIL TO SALT HEAT EXCHANGER SYSTEM INDICATES THE VESSEL IS FILLED WITH NITROGEN GAS FOR SHIPPING (SEE TRANSPORT DRAWING). ALL PERSONNEL MUST BE CAUTIONED NOT TO PUT THEIR HEAD INTO VESSEL OPENINGS TO INSPECT THE INTERIOR. NITROGEN IS COLORLESS AND ODORLESS AND SUFFOCATION COULD TAKE PLACE WITHOUT THE SENSES PROVIDING AN ALARM.

The Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System should be thoroughly inspected for shipping damage immediately upon arrival. Heaters with an internal nitrogen atmosphere have pressure gauges to show internal pressure on both shell and tube side. Heaters are shipped under 0.6 barg nitrogen pressure. If the nitrogen pressure has fallen below 0.10 barg during shipment, it should be restored between 0.10 and 0.6 barg.

Heaters should be stored on cribbing and not in contact with the ground. Horizontal heaters should rest on their supports. Heaters with a nitrogen atmosphere, once positioned, should be "blown down" by loosening a plug or nozzle cover at the low point and allowing any condensate to escape from each side of the heater. CAUTION IS TO BE EXERCISED WHEN LOOSENING THE PLUG, OR COVER, BECAUSE THE OIL T O SALT HEAT EXCHANGER SYSTEM IS PRESSURIZED. PERSONNEL SHOULDN'T HAVE ANY PORTION OF THEIR BODY IN DIRECT LINE WITH THE PLUG OR COVER. The opening should be immediately resealed. The loss of nitrogen will be very slight.

Nitrogen blanketed heaters should be inspected on a regular basis. If the pressure falls below 0.1 barg, it should be restored to 0.6 barg (max.). Minor leaks can be stopped by a slight uniform tightening of cover bolts or by tightening the threaded bar plugs in couplings or welded shipping covers. Refer to the instructions covering "Removal of Welded Shipping Covers" at the end of this section of the manual.

If uniform tightening of gasket cover bolts will not stop a leak, the gasket must be replaced to permit restoration of the nitrogen seal. To minimize the time a vessel is open, all tools and material should be available before starting the work.

**CAUTION:** BEFORE BEGINNING TO REMOVE THE BOLTS HOLDING A GASKET COVER, BLEED THE NITROGEN PRESSURE DOWN TO ZERO. DO NOT ATTEMPT TO REMOVE A COVER OR BAR PLUG FROM A PRESSURIZED VESSEL.

The recommended gasket material for pre-installation storage and nitrogen purge is 1/8" thick Garlock Blue-Gard service sheet or equal. The gasket should be cut to fit just inside the bolt circle. If the nozzle cover has a coupling in it, cut a one inch (1") hole in the gasket to match that location.

While the cover is removed, inspect it and the nozzle end for any surface condition that could cause a leak.

Replace the cover and new gasket as quickly as possible to minimize nitrogen loss. Secure the hold down bolts by finger-tightening and pressurize the vessel with nitrogen. Allow the gas to flow around the loose cover and seep out air admitted during the change. A five minute flow should restore the nitrogen atmosphere.

Tighten the cover uniformly in the conventional star pattern. Do not over-tighten and risk cutting the gasket. Retest with a soap solution or a commercial solution.

## 1.2. INSTALLATION INSTRUCTIONS

Each Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger is shipped completely assembled and ready for installation on the purchaser's supports (six equipment for train: three bottom equipment and three top equipment). The heater must be installed as shown on the General Arrangement Drawing in a true horizontal plane.

**GENERAL HANDLING INSTRUCTIONS**

Prior to any planned lifting of a heat exchanger, the rigging foreman should review the supplied Transport drawing. This drawing contains the necessary data to plan normal lifting of the exchanger. This information consists of the following items:

1. Empty weight of each exchanger.
2. Location of the longitudinal center of gravity.

**CAUTION:** DO NOT CONFUSE PULLING LUGS WITH LIFTING LUGS. PULLING LUGS WILL NOT SUPPORT THE VESSEL WEIGHT.

### TWO HOOK LIFT (HORIZONTAL VESSELS ONLY)

When using two hooks to lift a vessel, the vessel can be tilted lengthwise. This allows moving the vessel through hatchways and other areas that do not have sufficient clearance for the vessel to pass through in the level, horizontal position. Slings should be sized at 1.4142 times the vessel weight and the vessel should not be tilted according to shipping drawing.

**CAUTION:** STANDARD SAFETY RULES SHOULD BE ADHERED TO AT ALL TIMES DURING THE HANDLING OF A HEAT EXCHANGER. TO PREVENT POSSIBLE SHOCK DAMAGE, THE VESSEL SHOULD NOT BE ALLOWED TO "STRIKE" INTO ANY OBJECT. WHEN LOWERING INTO A RESTING POSITION THE VESSEL SHOULD NOT BE DROPPED OR BUMPED. SHIPPING COVERS SHOULD NOT BE REMOVED UNTIL THE VESSEL IS LOCATED IN ITS FINAL POSITION; THIS WILL PREVENT ANY UNDUE CONTAMINATION.

About the hoisting points, there is not any special or hook points; only take into account the following ones:

- Lifting operations must be with nylon slings. In case of steel slings the contact points must be protected with rubbers or similar.

Once the equipment is installed, all damaged painted parts should be repainted before starting insulation. All heaters are to be lifted into place. Eyebolts, lugs, clips or other devices are installed only to lift the empty components to which they are attached and not the assembled Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System, unless explicitly specified otherwise.

Proper rigging must be used in lifting assembled Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger Systems. Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger Systems are usually much heavier than pressure vessels of comparable dimensions; therefore, weights should be secured from the Transport Drawing.

# Installation, Start-Up, Operation and Maintenance Instructions

## Removal of Shipping Covers
- Remove shipping covers immediately before connections.
- Use grinding to remove welded-on steel caps.
- Bleed internal nitrogen pressure to zero before cutting off a cover.
- Discard covers; never salvage them.
- Contact LOINTEK for assistance with repairs or alterations.

## Hydrostatic Testing
- Perform hydrostat testing if required, see ANNEX 2.

## Start-Up and Operation
- Review plant operating instructions and drawings before operation.
- Avoid improper start-up or shutdown sequences to prevent leaks or tube breaks.
- Initial cold start-up involves filling with preheated HTF at a maximum ramp of 90ºF/hour (50ºC/hour).
- Warm start-up follows normal charge or discharge modes, limited by temperature fluctuations.

## Maintenance
- Ensure system is clean and free of foreign objects before operation.
- Do not expose internals to moisture or harmful contaminants.
- Use nitrogen for corrosion protection until piping installation is complete.
- Contact LOINTEK for any alterations or repairs.

# Maintenance & Repairs Recommendations

### Scope
This section provides recommendations for assembly and maintenance of external bolted joints utilizing gasketed sealing surfaces. Recommendations do not replace existing plant maintenance procedures.
### Preparation for Assembly
1. Clean all gasket joint surfaces.
2. Inspect gasket surfaces for defects or damage.
3. Dress out roughness, burrs, or surface irregularities.
4. Lubricate threads on studs and nuts.
### Assembly
1. Snuggle up the joint so the entire flange face bears uniformly on the gasket.

# LOCATING TUBE FAILURES

To determine the probable causes of tube failure, it is essential that the exact location of each failure be known.

This instruction gives a method of locating the point of failure in an Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger tube. Although other means may be employed, it has been our experience that the system described represents a practical procedure for making an accurate determination of the location of a tube failure.

When collecting data on tubing failures, the following minimum amount of information must be recorded:

1. LOINTEK Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System "Serial Number".
2. The pass in which the failure occurred (inlet or outlet).
3. Tube row, counted from pass plate.
4. Tube number, counted from the left when facing the tubesheet.
5. Distance of the failure back from face of tubesheet.
6. Length of failure, where possible.
7. Date of investigation.

## TUBESHEET/TUBE END EXAMINATION:

The face of the tubesheet, the tube ends and the inlet pass tube inside surfaces are to be visually examined using a bright light, from a distance of approximately 30cm. This examination should disclose any pitting, "bug holes", or other indications of damage.

If any tubes have been previously plugged, they must be checked to ensure no leak paths have developed around the plug.

Once the visual inspection is finished, it is recommended to make a penetrant test in the tubesheet surface. With this test it is possible to detect damages in the weldings between the tube and the tubesheet.

Other testing will be performed in order to find damages inside the tubes like bubble test.

After detecting the leaks a plug will be use in order to block the damaged tube. That plug is placed inside the tube and welded to the tubesheet according to the following drawing:

## TUBE PLUGGING:

These instructions cover the plugging of Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger tubes. Plugging is divided into the following categories, depending on the nature of the tube failure.

1. Tube-end Plugging: Where failure has occurred in the tube itself, and the tube-to-tubesheet joint is sound.
2. Safety Plugging: Where tubes are plugged because of possible erosion damage due to a failure in an adjacent tube.
3. Tubesheet Plugging: Where the tube-to-tubesheet joint has failed beyond repair, specific instructions will be issued by LOINTEK.

## GENERAL PREPARATION FOR TUBE PLUGGING:

Plugs conforming to the material and dimensions given in these instructions should be obtained from Lointek Procedure. Please contact LOINTEK should tube plugs be desired.

## GENERAL PRECAUTIONS WHEN USING WELDED PLUGS:

Weld wire substitutions are not to be made without prior approval by LOINTEK.

Do not weld until the welding area and the tubes, including plugs, are clean and dry. Clean the tube holes and plugs with a high grade, non-residue solvent. Tools, hoses, etc., in the work area must be clean.

Heat the area in the tubesheet to be welded, as required, to remove moisture.

The bar used for plugs that are to be welded must not be of a "free machining" type material.

## TESTS FOR PLUGGED TUBES:

Tube plug welds should be tested as follows prior to putting the equipment back in service.

1. Bubble leak test the plug by applying air or nitrogen at 5 barg on the shellside and applying a bubble forming solution on the plug to tubesheet joint.
2. A color contrast liquid penetrant examination is to be performed on welded plugs.
3. (OPTIONAL) Hydrostatic test the shellside at a pressure approximately 10% below the shellside design pressure stated on the nameplate. Any test pressure exceeding the design pressure will require gagging or blanking off the shell safety valve. Hold the test pressure for at least four hours, preferably overnight, and check for any droplets or weepage.

## BOLTED PASS PARTITION COVER PLATE REMOVAL AND REPLACEMENT:

Pass partition cover plates are fitted with either handles for handling purposes, or they have nuts welded to the cover plate that are used to attach bolts, bars, or eyes for the purpose of handling the cover. Where access to the channel internals and pass partition is restricted through a manway, pass partition covers are typically fitted with handles and can be manipulated by one person and removed through the manway. Where access to the channel internals and pass partition is through a full access channel cover, pass partition covers are typically fitted with nuts welded to the cover and are sized to be removable through the channel cover opening. These full access partition covers are normally larger segments that cannot be handled by one person without external help.

The following steps should be followed for removal and installation of the pass partition cover and gasket:

### REMOVAL:

1. Caution must be used when removing pass partition cover bolting to ensure that the cover plate are secured and will not fall free or shift when bolts are removed, possibly leading to injury of personnel or damage to equipment.
2. Remove the nuts holding the cover plate to remove the cover. Nuts are typically tack welded to the cover plate on one flat to prevent loosening during operation.
3. Remove the pass partition cover gasket.

### REPLACEMENT:

1. Visually inspect pass partition welds where pass partition attaches to the channel. If equipment is in cyclic service or if visual inspection results in the need for closer inspection, pass partition welds should be examined by liquid penetrant methods.
2. Clean and inspect the bolts and cover plate for damage such as erosion or "washing". If roughness or burrs are present, they should be dressed out to provide a smooth surface.
3. Threads on pass partition cover bolts shall be cleaned. Pass partition cover bolts are normally installed without thread lubricant.
4. Clean and inspect all gasket joint surfaces. Inspect gasket surfaces for defects or damage that may hinder proper sealing of the gasket.
5. Any suspect areas found as a result of the above examination should be repaired prior to installation of the pass partition cover.
6. Obtain new pass partition gasket.
7. Gaskets should be handled with care to avoid excessive bending during handling.
8. Re-install the pass partition gaskets, cover, and nuts.
a. Pass partition cover bolts are typically installed ‘dry’ with no lubricant.
b. Tightening of the bolts shall be applied in at least three equal increments to achieve the final torque. Tightening shall be applied in a staggered “star” pattern to ensure that the gasket is compressed uniformly and that gasket surface will not be distorted by uneven tightening.
d. Final torque value for AISI 304 bolts (see torque values table page 20).
e. Once final torque is achieved, tack weld one flat of each nut to cover to prevent loosening during operation.

# ANNEX 2: HYDROSTATIC TESTING

**LIMITATIONS:**
- Hydrostatic test pressures must not exceed those stated on the nameplate and General Arrangement drawing.
- Verify shellside and tubeside test pressure stamps before field hydrotests.
- Avoid water temperatures below 60°F (15.5°C).
- Check ASME code for minimum temperature requirements.
- Hold system filled with test water for one-half hour, then drain a sample to check temperature.

**CODE INSPECTION:**
- Recommended that hydrostatic tests be witnessed by the owner's Authorized Inspector and Notify Body.

**TESTING PRECAUTIONS:**
- Open start-up vents until all passages are filled with fluid.
- Close vents after filling and purging air or nitrogen.
- Apply pressure at a steady rate, avoiding sudden surges.
- Use a small capacity pump for hydrostatic testing.
- Refer to the hydrostatic test drawing before starting.

**DRAINAGE AFTER HYDROSTATIC TEST:**
- Drain the system after hydrostatic testing.
- Dry and nitrogen-blanket steel heaters following drainage.
