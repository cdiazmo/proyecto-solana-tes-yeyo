# Standby and Drainage Procedures for Shell and Tube HEX in TES System

## General Overview
- The document provides a functional description of the standby and drainage procedures for the Shell and Tube HEX in the TES (Thermal Energy Storage) system.
- The CCR (Central Control Room) is divided into three major operating stations: SF-HTF, SG-STG, and TES.
- Procedures are based on semi-automatic/manual operation.

## General Description of TES HEX
- TES HEX allows energy transfer between HTF (Heat Transfer Fluid) and molten salt.
- Six TES trains, each with six shell and tube heat exchangers, are arranged in series.
- Each train has its own drainage and vent lines controlled by manual and automatic valves.

## Instrumentation
- Pressure transmitters: PIT-X-255-01 to PIT-X-255-10
- Temperature transmitters: TIT-X-255-01 to TIT-X-255-10

## Molten Salt Drainage System
- The system evacuates molten salt from the HEX and piping, pumping it back to the MS Hot Tank.
- Molten salt drains reach the drain back tank by gravity with additional N2 supplied at the top of the Exchanger for enhanced drainage.
- Main components include the MS drain back tank (XX-MT-257) and pump (XX-MP-258).

## Notes
- The tags 'XX' in tank and pump TAGs are replaced with numbers 1, 2, or 3 depending on the TES system.

# TES HEX Drainage and Vent System Logic Control Description
## General Logic
The aim of this section is to compile and describe the control logic, interlocks, and formulas used to control the system. The signals used in the development of the control logic for the TES HEX drainage and vent system are contained in the Signal list document, which includes all alarm settings.
Every Signal (Autostarts / Autostops / Alarms / Interlocks) must be maintained during a specific time in the trip value before activation. This is used to avoid transitory peaks. These times will be established or confirmed during commissioning.
### Drain Back Pump
The drain back pump will be manually operated. The pump operates based on signals from LI-X-257-01, LI-X-251-01, and PI-X-257-02 with associated interlocks E1, E2, E3, and alarms A1, A2, and A3.
### On/Off Control Valves
#### Venting of Blanket Gas in the Drain Back Tank
The XV-X-257-13 valve is controlled by PI-X-257-01 with associated signals from DCS for open and close commands.
#### Molten Salt TES HEX Nitrogen to HEX Drains On/Off Valves
The XV-X-255-16, XV-X-255-19, and XV-X-255-21 valves are controlled by manual orders from the operator with associated signals from DCS for open and close commands.
#### Molten Salt TES HEX Venting for MS Filling
The XV-X-255-17, XV-X-255-20, and XV-X-255-23 valves are controlled by manual orders from the operator with associated signals from DCS for open and close commands.
#### Molten Salt Drain Back Pump Discharge Valve
The HV-X-257-14/15 valve is controlled based on the selector of the corresponding train with associated signals from DCS for open and close commands.
#### HTF TES HEX Vent On/Off Valves
The XV-X-255-15, XV-X-255-18, and XV-X-255-22 valves are controlled by manual orders from the operator with associated signals from DCS for open and close commands.
## Control Loop
### LIC-X-257-01: Control of Level at MS Drain Back Tank
The aim in this control loop is to maintain the level at the molten salt drain back tank. The control valve LV-X-257-01 opens or closes based on the level signal from LI-X-257-01 and set point value.

# Transition to Standby Sequence
## From Charge mode with HEX enclosure
### Initial condition:
- SF in tracking
- PB online or offline
- TES charging
When PB is online, TES is normally taken offline and brought to standby mode when STG load starts increasing and there isn't enough DNI to increase the HTF flow to the system.
## From Discharge with HEX enclosure
### Initial condition:
- PB online or offline (seals and vacuum operation)
- TES discharging
TES units that are in discharge operation may be supporting power generation or in hot freeze protection.
## HEX Drainage operation
When TES is on standby mode, it might be necessary to extend the shutdown for maintenance purposes. In these cases, a completely HEX drainage is recommended.
