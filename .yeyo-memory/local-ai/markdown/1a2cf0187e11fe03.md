# SECTION 1: SHIPPING AND INSTALLATION

## 1.1. PRE-INSTALLATION STORAGE OF OIL TO SALTS HEAT EXCHANGER SYSTEMS

**CAUTION:** A PRESSURE GAUGE ON AN OIL TO SALT HEAT EXCHANGER SYSTEM INDICATES THE VESSEL IS FILLED WITH NITROGEN GAS FOR SHIPPING (SEE TRANSPORT DRAWING). ALL PERSONNEL MUST BE CAUTIONED NOT TO PUT THEIR HEAD INTO VESSEL OPENINGS TO INSPECT THE INTERIOR. NITROGEN IS COLORLESS AND ODORLESS AND SUFFOCATION COULD TAKE PLACE WITHOUT THE SENSES PROVIDING AN ALARM.

The Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System should be thoroughly inspected for shipping damage immediately upon arrival. Exchangers with an internal nitrogen atmosphere have pressure gauges to show internal pressure on both shell and tube side. Heaters are shipped under 0.6 barg nitrogen pressure. If the nitrogen pressure has fallen below 0.10 barg during shipment, it should be restored between 0.10 and 0.6 barg.

Heaters should be stored on cribbing and not in contact with the ground. Horizontal heaters should rest on their supports. Heaters with a nitrogen atmosphere, once positioned, should be "blown down" by loosening a plug or nozzle cover at the low point and allowing any condensate to escape from each side of the heater. CAUTION IS TO BE EXERCISED WHEN LOOSENING THE PLUG, OR COVER, BECAUSE THE OIL TO SALT HEAT EXCHANGER SYSTEM IS PRESSURIZED. PERSONNEL SHOULD NOT HAVE ANY PORTION OF THEIR BODY IN DIRECT LINE WITH THE PLUG OR COVER. The opening should be immediately resealed. The loss of nitrogen will be very slight.

Nitrogen blanketed heaters should be inspected on a regular basis. If the pressure falls below 0.1 barg, it should be restored to 0.6 barg (max.). Minor leaks can be stopped by a slight uniform tightening of cover bolts or by tightening the threaded bar plugs in couplings or welded shipping covers. Refer to the instructions covering "Removal of Welded Shipping Covers" at the end of this section of the manual.

If uniform tightening of gasket cover bolts will not stop a leak, the gasket must be replaced to permit restoration of the nitrogen seal. To minimize the time a vessel is open, all tools and material should be available before starting the work.

**CAUTION:** BEFORE BEGINNING TO REMOVE THE BOLTS HOLDING A GASKET COVER, BLEED THE NITROGEN PRESSURE DOWN TO ZERO. DO NOT ATTEMPT TO REMOVE A COVER OR BAR PLUG FROM A PRESSURIZED VESSEL.

The recommended gasket material for pre-installation storage and nitrogen purge is 1/8" thick Garlock Blue-Gard service sheet or equal. The gasket should be cut to fit just inside the bolt circle. If the nozzle cover has a coupling in it, cut a one inch (1") hole in the gasket to match that location.

While the cover is removed, inspect it and the nozzle end for any surface condition that could cause a leak.

Replace the cover and new gasket as quickly as possible to minimize nitrogen loss. Secure the hold down bolts by finger-tightening and pressurize the vessel with nitrogen. Allow the gas to flow around the loose cover and seep out air admitted during the change. A five minute flow should restore the nitrogen atmosphere.

Tighten the cover uniformly in the conventional star pattern. Do not over-tighten and risk cutting the gasket. Retest with a soap solution or a commercial solution.

Each Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger is shipped completely assembled and ready for installation on the purchaser's supports (six equipments for train: three bottom equipments and three top equipments). The heater must be installed as shown on the General Arrangement Drawing in a true horizontal plane.

Prior to any planned lifting of a heat exchanger, the rigging foreman should review the supplied Transport drawing. This drawing contains the necessary data to plan normal lifting of the exchanger. This information consists of the following items:

1. Empty weight of each exchanger.
2. Location of the longitudinal center of gravity.

**CAUTION:** DO NOT CONFUSE PULLING LUGS WITH LIFTING LUGS. PULLING LUGS WILL NOT SUPPORT THE VESSEL WEIGHT.

When using two hooks to lift a vessel, the vessel can be tilted lengthwise. This allows moving the vessel through hatchways and other areas that do not have sufficient clearance for the vessel to pass through in the level, horizontal position. Slings should be sized at 1.4142 times the vessel weight and the vessel should not be tilted according to shipping drawing.

**CAUTION:** STANDARD SAFETY RULES SHOULD BE ADHERED TO AT ALL TIMES DURING THE HANDLING OF A HEAT EXCHANGER. TO PREVENT POSSIBLE SHOCK DAMAGE, THE VESSEL SHOULD NOT BE ALLOWED TO "STRIKE" INTO ANY OBJECT. WHEN LOWERING INTO A RESTING POSITION THE VESSEL SHOULD NOT BE DROPPED OR BUMPED. SHIPPING COVERS SHOULD NOT BE REMOVED UNTIL THE VESSEL IS LOCATED IN ITS FINAL POSITION; THIS WILL PREVENT ANY UNDUE CONTAMINATION.

# Installation, Start-Up, Operation and Maintenance Instructions
## Heat Exchanger System Rigging
- Proper rigging must be used for lifting assembled Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger Systems.
- Weights should be secured from the Transport Drawing due to their heavy nature compared to pressure vessels of similar dimensions.
## Setting Heaters
- Fixed supports require shims for proper elevation and orientation.
- Foundation bolts in slotted support holes must have nuts loosened for free expansion of the shell.
- Anchor bolt stakeout should match equipment drill distribution, as per the "anchor bolts drawing".
- Sliding support stainless steel plate must be installed with top elevations matching fixed support nuts.
## Equipment Alignment and Leveling
- Before lifting any equipment, verify KKS on nameplate matches field location.
- All bolt elements (washers, bushings, nuts, locknuts) must be correctly installed before lifting.
- Ensure both saddles of lower equipment are at the same elevation before lifting upper equipment.
## Piping Connections
- Remove shipping covers and plugs immediately prior to piping connections.
- Residual water inside the heater should be removed through nozzles.
- Nitrogen is colorless and odorless, so caution must be exercised when visually inspecting internals.
- All connections on the General Arrangement Drawing must be utilized unless otherwise approved by LOINTEK Engineering Department.
- Pipelines should be blown before welding to equipment.
## Corrosion Protection
- Oil to Salt Heat Exchangers are protected against corrosion with nitrogen, so they should remain sealed as long as possible before piping installation.
- After piping is complete, the heater should be immediately protected with a nitrogen blanket or other suitable means of protection against corrosion.
## Alterations or Repairs
- Any alterations or repairs during installation, testing, or operation must be made under LOINTEK direction and in accordance with LOINTEK procedures.
## Hydrostatic Testing
- If field hydrostat testing is to be performed, see ANNEX 2.
## Removal of Welded Shipping Covers
- LOINTEK covers weld end nozzles with welded-on steel caps for protection.
- Bleed internal nitrogen pressure to zero before cutting off a shipping cover.
- Grinding is the intended method of removal, starting at the edge of the cap and breaking the cap to weld joint.

# Start-Up and Operation Instructions for Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System

## Section 2: START-UP AND OPERATION

Starting, operating, and shutting down an Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System require specific instructions, with emphasis on load limitations and the order of start-up and shut-down. The plant operating instructions and the Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System Drawing, specification sheet, and nameplates are to be reviewed prior to operating the system.

### Precautions
Improper starting-up or incorrect shutdown sequence can cause leaks in tube-plate weldings, flanged joints of equipment, or even a tube breakage.

### Operation Modes
- **Initial Start-Up (Filling)**
- **Warm Start-up (Normal Start Up)**
- **Hot Stat-up & Standby mode**
- **Shutdown**

## 2.1 Initial Cold Start-Up

During the initial start-up of the plant, heat exchangers will be subjected to specific sequences:

- Initially empty and at ambient temperature.
- Electric heat tracing activated until 50ºC with a heating rate of 1,8ºF/hour (1ºC/hour).
- Preheated HTF introduced at 176ºF (80ºC) with a maximum ramp of 90ºF/hour (50ºC/hour) up to 545ºF (285ºC).
- Salt introduced at ± 27ºF (15ºC) from the previous temperature.

## 2.2 Warm Start-Up (Normal Charge or Discharge)

For normal charge or discharge modes, follow the operation limits specified in 4521-MOM-LO-039-062-0003-GAR.

### Operating Conditions and Performance
Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System operating conditions and performance should be checked regularly against the values stated on the specification sheet. Significant deviations should be reported immediately to LOINTEK.

## 2.3 Shutdown
The shutdown must stop hot fluid first, then cold side flow. For extended periods, protect internals from corrosion by blanketing with nitrogen or another inert gas or flooding with oxygen-free condensate if an inert gas is not available.

### DRAIN
First fluid to drain should be salts. No pressure difference limitations apart from design pressures. Temperature ramp limited to 18ºF/minute (10ºC/min). Introduce nitrogen at highest points of the exchanger for easier salt drainage.

## 3.2. SHELL REMOVAL AND REPLACEMENT
### SCOPE
These instructions cover the removal and replacement of tube bundles in an Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger System.
### GENERAL
Care must be exercised to ensure that the bundle is always evenly supported during removal or insertion.
- Shell is pulled off using pulling lugs in steps, providing support for the bundle at each step.
- Cribbing must be used to raise the weight of the bundle off the shell before clearing the last support plate.
- On horizontal heaters, cribbing must be placed at a maximum spacing of 2m and long enough to carry the full width of the flat bottom of the support plates.
### REINSTALLATION OF SHELL (BUNDLE INSERTION)
Prior to insertion, a thorough inspection is required. The bundle must be checked for alignment and cleanliness.
- Shell is drawn over the bundle by placing a cable around the forward shell support and pulling from the channel end.
- An air operated hoist is generally used for bundle insertion.
- Mechanic must observe the open end of the shell passing across support plates and bundle skids.

## ASSEMBLY
All bolted connections are to be tightened uniformly in a diametrically staggered pattern as shown on the Bolt Tightening Pattern Sketch. Tightening to the listed torque values shall be achieved in multiple steps as noted below, with each tightening increment performed in the same diametrically staggered pattern.

1. The joint shall be snuggled up squarely so the entire flange face bears uniformly on the gasket.
2. Tightening of the bolts shall be applied in at least three equal increments to achieve torque values listed in the following torque table. It is suggested that 4 equal increments be used for bolt diameters above 2”.
3. Alternatively, bolts can tighten one flat at a time in a diametrically staggered pattern until the specified torque is achieved.

If the bolted connection leaks, the nuts can be tightened in the pattern outlined on the Bolt Tightening Pattern Sketch, one flat at a time. The spacing between the flanges must be equal throughout the tightening process. This will ensure the gasket surface will not be distorted.

If a connection continues to leak, it must be disassembled and examined for the following:
- Indication of a leak path, such as a gouge across the gasket surface.
- Evidence of interference between the mating connections.
- Evidence of distortion or out of roundness of either of the mating gasket surfaces.
- Evidence of bolt diameter reduction or "necking", which would indicate an overstressing of the bolts.

In order to ensure that all bolted flanged joints remain tight, such joints should be tightened uniformly immediately after the heater has been placed in service for the first time, and again after the heater has been operated at full load for the first time. It is recommended that periodic checks be made during the first six months of operation to ensure proper tightness of all bolted joints.

## GASKETS
Gasket selection should be in accordance with the original design. Please contact LOINTEK if gaskets are required. Diaphragm gaskets don't have to be replaced. The use of a non-hardening compound is recommended to aid in holding the gasket in place during bolt-up. The use of masking tape or a hardening compound is not recommended. Gasket surfaces must be inspected for cleanliness and absence of scratches, erosion, etc., before gaskets are installed. If there is any evidence of damage on gasket surfaces, or if there is any question with regard to gasket material and/or type, this should also be referred to LOINTEK.

When a jacketed type gasket is used in conjunction with a nubbin (raised ring) on one of the gasket surfaces, the flat side of the gasket must be in contact with the nubbin.

## TUBESHEET/TUBE END EXAMINATION
The face of the tubesheet, the tube ends and the inlet pass tube inside surfaces are to be visually examined using a bright light, from a distance of approximately 30cm. This examination should disclose any pitting, "bug holes", or other indications of damage.

If any tubes have been previously plugged, they must be checked to ensure no leak paths have developed around the plug.

Once the visual inspection is finished, it is recommended to make a penetrant test in the tubesheet surface. With this test it is possible to detect damages in the weldings between the tube and the tubesheet.

Other testing will be performed in order to find damages inside the tubes like bubble test.

# TUBE PLUGGING
These instructions cover the plugging of Oil to Salt Heat Exchanger tubes. Plugging is divided into the following categories, depending on the nature of the tube failure.
1. Tube-end Plugging: Where failure has occurred in the tube itself, and the tube-to-tubesheet joint is sound.
2. Safety Plugging: Where tubes are plugged because of possible erosion damage due to a failure in an adjacent tube.
3. Tubesheet Plugging: Where the tube-to-tubesheet joint has failed beyond repair, specific instructions will be issued by LOINTEK.

**GENERAL PREPARATION FOR TUBE PLUGGING.**
Plugs conforming to the material and dimensions given in these instructions should be obtained from Lointek Procedure. Please contact LOINTEK should tube plugs be desired.

**GENERAL PRECAUTIONS WHEN USING WELDED PLUGS:**
Weld wire substitutions are not to be made without prior approval by LOINTEK.
Do not weld until the welding area and the tubes, including plugs, are clean and dry. Clean the tube holes and plugs with a high grade, non-residue solvent. Tools, hoses, etc., in the work area must be clean. Heat the area in the tubesheet to be welded, as required, to remove moisture. The bar used for plugs that are to be welded must not be of a "free machining" type material.

**TESTS FOR PLUGGED TUBES:**
1. Bubble leak test the plug by applying air or nitrogen at 5 barg on the shellside and applying a bubble forming solution on the plug to tubesheet joint.
2. A color contrast liquid penetrant examination is to be performed on welded plugs.
3. (OPTIONAL) Hydrostatic test the shellside at a pressure approximately 10% below the shellside design pressure stated on the nameplate. Any test pressure exceeding the design pressure will require gagging or blanking off the shell safety valve. Hold the test pressure for at least four hours, preferably overnight, and check for any droplets or weepage.

# BOLTED PASS PARTITION COVER PLATE REMOVAL AND REPLACEMENT
Pass partition cover plates are fitted with either handles for handling purposes, or they have nuts welded to the cover plate that are used to attach bolts, bars, or eyes for the purpose of handling the cover. Where access to the channel internals and pass partition is restricted through a manway, pass partition covers are typically fitted with handles and can be manipulated by one person and removed through the manway. Where access to the channel internals and pass partition is through a full access channel cover, pass partition covers are typically fitted with nuts welded to the cover and are sized to be removable through the channel cover opening. These full access partition covers are normally larger segments that cannot be handled by one person without external help.

The following steps should be followed for removal and installation of the pass partition cover and gasket:
**REMOVAL:**
1. Caution must be used when removing pass partition cover bolting to ensure that the cover plate are secured and will not fall free or shift when bolts are removed, possibly leading to injury of personnel or damage to equipment.
2. Remove the nuts holding the cover plate to remove the cover. Nuts are typically tack welded to the cover plate on one flat to prevent loosening during operation.
3. Remove the pass partition cover gasket.
**REPLACEMENT:**
1. Visually inspect pass partition welds where pass partition attaches to the channel. If equipment is in cyclic service or if visual inspection results in the need for closer inspection, pass partition welds should be examined by liquid penetrant methods.
2. Clean and inspect the bolts and cover plate for damage such as erosion or "washing". If roughness or burrs are present, they should be dressed out to provide a smooth surface
3. Threads on pass partition cover bolts shall be cleaned. Pass partition cover bolts are normally installed without thread lubricant.
4. Clean and inspect all gasket joint surfaces. Inspect gasket surfaces for defects or damage that may hinder proper sealing of the gasket.
5. Any suspect areas found as a result of the above examination should be repaired prior to installation of the pass partition cover.
6. Obtain new pass partition gasket.
7. Gaskets should be handled with care to avoid excessive bending during handling.

# ANNEX 2: HYDROSTATIC TESTING

**LIMITATIONS:**
- Hydrostatic test pressures must not be exceeded. Verify the test pressure stamped on the nameplate.
- Hydrostatic testing with water temperature less than 60°F (15.5°C) is not recommended. Check ASME code for minimum temperature.
- After filling, hold for one-half hour, drain a sample, and check temperature.

**CODE INSPECTION:**
- Recommended to witness hydrostatic tests by the owner's Authorized Inspector and Notify Body.

**TESTING PRECAUTIONS:**
- Open start-up vents until all passages are filled with fluid.
- Close vents after filling and purging air or nitrogen.
- Apply pressure at a steady rate, avoiding sudden surges.
- Use a small capacity pump for hydrostatic testing.
- Review the hydrostatic test drawing before starting.

**DRAINAGE AFTER HYDROSTATIC TEST:**
- Drain the system after testing and dry/nitrogen blanket if necessary.
