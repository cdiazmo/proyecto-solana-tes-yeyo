## Personnel Protection (PP)
Insulation shall be installed where called for by the insulation drawings and specifications. It shall be provided to a height of 7 feet above grade, platform and walkway and to a horizontal distance of 2 feet from a walkway or platform. The surface skin temperature will be limited to 160°F.

## Install Materials in Strict Accordance with Manufacturer’s Instructions

## Surface Condition of Items to be Insulated
### Surfaces to be insulated shall be clean and dry. The Contractor shall be responsible for the removal of oil, grease, dust, and similar accumulations of surface dirt.
### Contractor will not be held responsible for the removal of mill scale, rust scale or deposits, old paint, or similar firmly adhering accumulations, but he shall report these conditions to the Abener / Teyma Construction Superintendent.

## Workmanship
### All insulating work shall be performed in a neat and workmanlike manner, of high quality, and consistent with current, acceptable standards of the industry. Contractor shall take into proper account the particular service and temperature of the systems included in this project. Finished installation shall be weatherproof, durable and sound, without developing cracks, separation, defects or burning, etc., due to misapplication of materials or workmanship. Contractor shall follow manufacturer’s installation instructions.
### Insulation shall be applied with care. Due regard shall be given to various factors including expansion, contraction, wind and water damage, frost prevention, breathing of hot insulation, weatherproofing, and overall appearance and long life.

### Insulation and jacketing must be applied in a manner that will allow for the required thermal expansion, of both the equipment and the insulated item, without creating gaps and hot spots in the insulation or damaging the jacketing and creating water leaks.

### Wet or damp insulation shall not be used. It shall be replaced with dry insulation or be thoroughly dried before proceeding with the installation. Dried Insulation shall be approved by Abener / Teyma Construction Superintendent.

### Insulation and jacketing shall be protected from mechanical damage during construction. Damage by the Contractor shall be repaired at Contractor’s expense. Damage by others shall be reported in writing to the Abener / Teyma Construction Superintendent.

### Finished edges of jacketing surfaces which meet or butt to dissimilar materials shall have a neat, clean and sharp appearance.

### Equipment nameplates and code inspection plates shall not be covered. Such openings shall be finished in a neat manner, with edges sealed with a jacket or covering to prevent the entering of any moisture.

### Use of pre-formed or molded pipe insulation segments for vessels with a diameter up to 42” maximum shall be considered to be acceptable.

### Equipment & piping that is insulated at grade, prior to installation and prior to application of the protective cover, shall be properly covered to prevent absorption of rainwater by the insulation.

### All insulation work, prior to application of the aluminum protective cover, shall be properly weather protected to prevent absorption of rainwater by the insulation.

### All areas between dissimilar metals shall be protected against galvanic corrosion by a suitable insulating coating.

## Section 7.3 Installation
- Finish installation with systems at operating conditions.
- Repair joints separations and cracking due to thermal movement.
- Repair damaged insulation facings by applying same facing material over damages areas.
- Extend patches at least 4" beyond damaged areas.
- Adhere, staple, and seal patches similar to butt joints.
- Construction Superintendent has final approval over quality and completeness/approval of all “Work”.

## Section 7.4 General Application Guidelines
- Use pre-formed, molded, or shaped insulation segments when available.
- Insulation should be applied in standard length sections, preferably 36" when possible.
- Insulation up to 3" thick may be applied in a single layer; thicker insulation requires two layers or more.
- All insulation must have staggered joint construction, fit snugly, and edges tightly butted with at least 2” overlap.
- Fill voids around gussets, manholes, nozzles, expansion joints, etc., with loose insulation.
- Manway and access way covers should be insulated separately.
- Provide expansion joints to accommodate thermal expansion differences.

## Section 7.4.8 Insulation Expansion Joints
- Provided at approximately 25-foot intervals when uninterrupted by fittings, flanges or valves.
- Consist of a 1-1/2" space packed with loose mineral wool, retained by the insulation jacket.

## Section 7.4.9 Insulation Supports
- Provided at 25-foot intervals on long uninterrupted vertical lines.
- Supports may be in the form of split rings clamped to pipe, spacing coinciding with expansion joints.

## Section 7.4.10 Flanges and Valves
- Flanges should be insulated unless otherwise specified.
- Flange insulation must be removable without damaging adjacent insulation.
- Pipeline insulation at flanges should terminate far enough away for removal of nuts and bolts without damaging the insulation and have weatherproofed ends.

## Section 7.4.11 Insulation on Flanges and Valves
- Flanges and valves should be insulated with oversized pipe insulation, fabricated in two halves.
- Neatly apply and wire or band firmly in place; overlap adjoining pipe insulation by not less than two times the thickness of pipe insulation or one pipe diameter, whichever is greater.

## Section 7.4.12 Insulation on Valves
- Use preformed fitting insulation or sectional pipe insulation of same material, density, and thickness as adjacent pipe.
- Overlap adjoining pipe insulation by not less than two times the thickness of pipe insulation or one pipe diameter, whichever is greater.

## Section 7.4.13 Insulation on Valve Bodies
- Insulate up to the packing gland; should not interfere with adjustment or removal of the packing gland and be sealed with a vapor barrier coating.

## Section 7.4.14 Insulation on Pipe Elbows
- Use preformed mitered fitting insulation made from same material and density as adjacent pipe insulation.
- Each piece butted tightly against adjoining piece and bonded with adhesive; when preformed fittings are not available, install mitered sections of pipe insulation to a thickness equal to adjoining pipe insulation. Secure with wire or bands.

## Insulation Specifications

### 7.4.15 Tee Fittings
- Insulate with preformed insulation or sectional pipe insulation matching adjacent pipe material and thickness.
- Cut sectional pipe insulation to fit and bond with adhesive.

### 7.4.16 Hangers
- Insulate hangers in the same manner as fittings, extending at least 3" beyond pipeline insulation and sealing off.

### 7.4.17 Stainless Steel Wire/Bands
- Use for securing insulation to piping, spaced 9” to 12” apart.

### 7.4.18 Insulation Extent
- Extend up to gauge isolation valves or chemical seal diaphragms.

### 7.4.19 Equipment Insulation
- Apply close to equipment by grooving, scoring, and beveling insulation.

### 7.4.20 Exposed Equipment
- Locate insulation and cover seams in least visible locations.

### 7.4.21 Joint Filling
- Fill joints, crack seams, and depressions with bedding compound; use vapor barrier cement on cold equipment.

### 7.4.22 Vapor Retarder-Faced Units
- Set units with vapor retarder to warm side of construction unless otherwise indicated.

### 7.4.23 Tape Joints/Ruptures
- Tape joints and ruptures in vapor retarder to surrounding construction for airtight installation.

## Non-code Items

1. Insulation over stiffeners: Apply over 1-1/2” x 13 ga. un-flattened, expanded metal secured with #10 ga. weld pins and speed clips.
2. Insulation on surfaces: Impale on #10 ga. weld pins on 12” centers and secure with speed clips; bend excess pin length below insulation surface.
3. Insulation fasteners: Same material as duct, vessel, or equipment they attach to.
4. Insulation over painted stainless steel: Use stainless steel bands and lacing wire if necessary.

## ASME Coded Items

1. Field welding on coded items requires re-certification; field welding is not allowed.
2. Insulation on cylindrical surfaces: Secure with stainless steel strapping bands, 1/2” x .015” for piping and 3/4” x .020” for vessels.
3. Strapping bands applied along vessel shell or pipe at 12” centers.

## Insulation Support Rings for Vertical Vessels and Piping
- Circumferential “clamp-on” insulation support rings are required for vertical vessels and piping.
- Radial bands should be applied on vessel heads in a uniform pattern with spacing of approximately 12" around the shell circumference.
- A floating ring made of ¼” diameter steel rod is placed at the center of the vessel head, minimizing its diameter.
- Two closely spaced bands are placed approximately 3” beyond the head tangent line over the insulation to form a girth around the periphery of the vessel.
- Radial bands should be fastened between the floating ring and the girth bands.
- All bands should be spring type, except for the girth bands which must be tightly secured to resist pull from radial bands.
- Code nameplates shall not be covered with insulation.

## Removable/Reusable Insulating Jacket
### Insulating Blankets and Pads
- Used on covers for manways and access ways, large and irregular shapes like duct “blank-offs”, gas tight and spade type, and HRS diluters.
- Shaped to the area to be insulated, cut and slotted for necessary penetrations, mated with lacing hooks and wire.
- Thickness over 3” to be applied in two layers.

### Formed and Shaped Covers
- Used on smaller items such as valves, flanges, strainers, etc.
- Covers should have the same appearance as adjacent permanent insulation, overlap and fit to it.
- Covers for valves shall not cover the packing and stem; they shall remain exposed.

### Insulated Metal Covers
- Metal covers enclosing flanges or other connections as well as the body of equipment to be insulated.
- Constructed with lock joints and lined with attached insulation, strong enough to withstand vibration and handling.
- Sections fastened with sheet metal screws or rivets, sealer applied as required.
- Removable/reusable insulating covers should be removed without damage, provide a weatherproof seal, and shed water.

## General Application Guidelines - Aluminum Jacketing
- **7.6.1:** Minimum overlaps for horizontal and circumferential seams: 3”
- **7.6.2:** Reinforce slits with plain aluminum and stainless steel sheet metal screws; minimum backup overlap: 3”
- **7.6.3:** Flash and seal openings to be weatherproof
- **7.6.4:** Apply jacketing when insulated work is in cold condition (ambient temperature)
- **7.6.5:** Insulation clip brackets must match the material of the vessel or equipment
- **7.6.6:** Inspection port and manhole cover jacketing should be removable, reusable, weatherproof, and shed water
- **7.6.7:** Use stainless steel screws with neoprene washers on longitudinal joints; spacing not to exceed 9”
- **7.6.8:** Stainless steel strapping bands for vessels, ducts, and piping; one breather/expansion spring per 25 feet of banding
- **7.6.9:** Use stainless steel “J” clips on vessels over 10’-0” in diameter where bands are not supported by “S” clips
- **7.6.10:** Use stainless steel “S” clips on large vertical ducts, vessels, and equipment; spacing about 12”, minimum of two per sheet
- **7.6.11:** Deep corrugated sheets overlapped a minimum of 2 full corrugations with stainless steel screws and neoprene washers
- **7.6.12:** Orange peel segments of head jackets overlapped, sealed with mastic, secured with stainless steel screws and neoprene washers; overlap the side jacket, minimum 3”
- **7.6.13:** Screws not to be used as fasteners on heat traced items
